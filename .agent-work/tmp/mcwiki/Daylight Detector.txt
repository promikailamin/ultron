A daylight detector (also known as a daylight sensor) is a block that outputs a redstone signal based on its exposure to sky light.
Using the detector inverts it, causing it instead to output a constant strong signal that weakens from exposure to sky light.


== Obtaining ==


=== Breaking ===
Daylight detectors can be mined by hand or with any tool, but axes are the quickest. Inverted daylight detectors cannot be collected directly; they drop a regular daylight detector. In Bedrock Edition, the inverted detector may be obtained with pick block in Creative mode, or via inventory editing or add-ons.

== Usage ==


=== Redstone component ===

A daylight detector is 0.375 blocks high (3/8ths of a block). In Java Edition Daylight detectors cannot be moved by pistons. Water and lava flow around daylight detectors without affecting them.
A Daylight detector can be changed to its inverted state by using it.


==== Activation ====
A daylight detector activates when exposed to sufficient daylight (default mode) or when exposure to daylight is low enough (inverted mode). Placing blocks that partially or fully block sky light anywhere above a daylight detector affects its output signal.
Sources of block light (torches, glowstone, etc.) cannot activate a daylight detector.


==== Behavior ====
An active daylight detector emits weak power in all six directions.
The output signal of daylight detectors is updated once every second (specifically, it's updated whenever gametime‌[Java Edition  only] or daytime‌[Bedrock Edition  only] is perfectly divisible by 20). It can also be manually updated by using the daylight detector.


==== Default mode ====
In the Overworld, a daylight detector's output signal strength depends on the time of day, the weather, the detector's exposure to the sky, and the internal sky light level.

A non-inverted daylight detector never produces a signal in the Nether and the End.


==== Inverted mode ====
Unlike what the name might imply, the output of the inverted detector is not a simple inversion of the daylight detector's output: a much simpler algorithm that depends on the internal sky light level is used instead. Specifically, it outputs a signal strength of 15 minus the current internal sky light level, where values over 15 or below 0 are taken as 15 or 0 respectively. This means that, in the Overworld, an inverted daylight detector actually outputs a strength of 11 at midnight when the weather is clear, if it has line of sight with the sky. The effects of shade are applied before inverting, so shade increases the signal strength when it is not already full, and prevents it from reaching zero. Completely blocking an inverted daylight detector from exposure to the sky causes it to emit a signal strength of 15 at all times of day.
in Java Edition, inverted daylight detectors in the Nether always produce a signal strength of 15. In the End, they work identically to how they work in the Overworld at noon in clear weather conditions.
in Bedrock Edition, inverted daylight detectors in the Nether or End never produce a signal under any circumstance.
The cells on inverted daylight detectors are emissive if Vibrant Visuals is enabled.


=== Fuel ===
Daylight detectors can be used as a fuel in furnaces, smelting 1.5 items per block.

=== Block data ===
A daylight detector has a block entity associated with it which stores only its entity ID and position (the minimum data for a block entity). Although the daylight detector's block entity stores no additional data, the block entity ensures that the daylight detector is updated every game tick.
